# Graphic Design — Core Elements & Skills

Source: [Figma — What is graphic design](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/what-is-graphic-design/)

## Why it matters

It's easy to assume graphic design is only about aesthetics, but at its core graphic
design is about *clarity*. Great design guides attention, tells a story, and makes
information easier to understand — the visual appeal is in service of that, not the
point of it.

## The eight elements

- **Color** — creates cohesion and impact, evokes emotion, communicates the intended
  message. Not decoration; a message-carrying choice.
- **Texture** — adds dimension, changes the overall feel of a design.
- **Typography** — font, size, weight, and arrangement together determine both
  readability and visual appeal. Treat readability as the constraint, appeal as the
  bonus.
- **Line** — straight, curved, thick, or thin — the foundation of graphic design.
  Lines create shapes and define space, and actively guide where the eye goes next.
- **Shape** — geometric or free-flowing; represents objects, creates contrast, and
  establishes hierarchy independent of color or size.
- **Space** — negative space (empty) and positive space (filled) create balance and
  naturally draw the eye toward what's important. Empty space is doing work, not
  wasted area.
- **Balance** — the distribution of visual weight, creating a sense of stability.
  Symmetrical balance mirrors elements (calm, formal); asymmetrical balance varies
  size, color, and texture across the composition (more dynamic, more visual
  interest).
- **Harmony** — establishes a cohesive visual identity; all elements share enough
  characteristics (palette, type system, spacing logic) that the whole reads as one
  thing rather than a collection of parts.

## Graphic design vs. UX design

They overlap — both use layout, color, and hierarchy to communicate clearly — but they
optimize for different things. **Graphic design** is about visual communication and
storytelling: color theory, typography, white space, composition, applied to static
visuals (print, banners, editorial, marketing materials). **UX design** is about how
people *interact* with a product: usability, flow, whether an interaction feels
intuitive, prioritizing accessibility and inclusion across screens and needs.

Worked example (a travel booking company): a graphic designer builds the email
banner promoting a discounted destination — high-quality imagery and typography to
grab attention. A UX/UI designer builds the *app feature* that lets someone actually
find that discount — a search filter, a push notification system. Same company, same
campaign, genuinely different skill sets and success criteria.

## Skills that make this work

- **Problem-solving** — research the target audience, then design toward their actual
  needs rather than a generic solution.
- **Storytelling** — color palettes, icons, illustrations, and layout convey a
  narrative; symbolism is a powerful tool for communicating a brand's story through a
  single image.
- **Design principles** — balance, contrast, hierarchy, movement, white space: the
  structural vocabulary that makes a design both appealing *and* functional, not one
  at the expense of the other.
- **Communication** — translating complex concepts or data sets into easy-to-digest
  graphics (a flowchart using shape, line, and type is a communication tool before it's
  a decoration).
- **Color theory** — how color works both emotionally and practically; used to build
  visual harmony, set mood, and direct attention.

## Five types of graphic design (and what each optimizes for)

1. **Branding & visual identity** — a cohesive image across all touchpoints: logo,
   typography, imagery, iconography, color palette, brand guidelines. Example: Dropbox's
   simple open-box logo and calming blue convey ease and reliability — the visual
   choices are doing semantic work, not just looking nice.
2. **Marketing design** — captures attention and drives engagement across email,
   social, ads, web assets, print, newsletters, flyers. A software launch campaign
   might use banners for feature awareness, then different layouts for each campaign
   stage — teaser, launch announcement, free-trial offer — each with a distinct job.
3. **Publication design** — layout for print (magazines, books, newspapers), with
   heavy emphasis on balance, hierarchy, and white space to guide the reader. National
   Geographic's use of captions, sidebars, and infographics alongside photography is
   the reference example of text and visuals working together.
4. **Illustration** — hand-drawn or digital visual interpretation of a concept, tuned
   to match brand voice. Headspace's illustrations reflect its focus on calm and
   mental well-being — the *style* of illustration is itself a brand signal.
5. **Packaging design** — influences purchasing decisions directly; uses color,
   imagery, and typography plus information hierarchy to surface key product info.
   Dove's soft palette (white/blue/beige) reflects a self-care positioning — again,
   color choice as message, not decoration.

## How this feeds SEO / performance (worth remembering for web work)

- Optimized images improve load speed, which search engines reward.
- Clear visual hierarchy reduces bounce rate by helping visitors find information
  fast.
- Responsive/mobile-friendly graphics are both more accessible and rank better.
- Consistent branding builds trust, which makes marketing messages more memorable.
